Personal Development Reflection (Twelve Pillars: Chapters 4–7)

From Chapters 4–7 of Twelve Pillars by Jim Rohn and Chris Widener, the strongest lesson I took away is that real growth doesn’t happen by accident—it comes from intentional habits and personal responsibility. The chapters emphasize building the “inner foundation” first: discipline, time awareness, and choosing better daily routines. In my life, this connects directly to how I approach school. When I plan ahead, study consistently, and stay organised, my results improve and I feel less pressure near deadlines.

Another important point is the idea of improving through small, consistent actions instead of waiting for motivation. I can align this by setting weekly targets (for coding practice, assignments, and personal learning), tracking my progress, and reflecting on what needs to change. Even when I’m tired or stressed, small effort still builds momentum. Over time, those habits support my long-term goal of becoming a software engineer who can handle complex work with focus, structure, and confidence.

Action Plan (How I Will Apply It)

  1. Create a weekly checklist for each course (CIT2011, Programming, etc.).
  2. Do at least 30 minutes of coding practice daily (even if it’s just small improvements).
  3. Review my work before submission (links, file paths, validation, layout).
  4. Reflect each weekend: what worked, what didn’t, and what to fix next week.
